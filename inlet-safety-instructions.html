Generator connection operating guide

Operating your generator connection

Keep this guide near your electrical panel. Follow the order shown every time you connect or disconnect your portable generator.

Keep the generator outdoors, at least 20 feet from the home.

Keep it away from windows, doors, and vents, with exhaust pointed away from the home. Use working carbon monoxide alarms on every level.

20 ft or more

Connecting during an outage

Begin with the generator and output breaker off.

1

Place it outdoors

Set the generator at least 20 feet from the home. Point exhaust away from every opening.

2

Lower the load

Turn off high-draw breakers such as AC, range, dryer, and electric water heater.

3

Connect while off

With the generator and its output breaker off, secure both ends of the supplied cord.

4

Start the generator

Follow its owner manual and let it stabilize. Turn its output breaker on, if equipped.

5

Transfer at the panel

Turn MAIN off, move the interlock, then turn the GENERATOR breaker on.

6

Add circuits slowly

Turn on what you need, one breaker at a time, while staying within generator capacity.

Important: Never connect or disconnect an energized cord. Do not force, alter, or bypass the interlock.
When utility power returns
Turn off the house circuits powered by the generator.
Generator breaker off, move interlock, then main breaker on.
Turn generator output off and stop it using its manual.
After it stops and cools, disconnect the cord. Refuel only when cool.
Do not
×
Run a generator indoors, in a garage, shed, or covered space.
×
Feed power through a wall outlet or any unapproved connection.
×
Alter, remove, or bypass the installed interlock.
×
Connect or disconnect the cord while it is energized.
×
Refuel a running or hot generator.

Generator compatibility and service scope

BPP installs and supports the house-side inlet, interlock, and wiring. Your portable generator is separate equipment.

Before first use

Confirm the generator is compatible with the installed system.

1

Use the supplied 4-wire cord. The generator outlet and house inlet may use different connector types. Do not improvise adapters.

2

A floating-neutral, or nonbonded, generator is required for this BPP inlet and interlock system unless your project documents state otherwise.

3

Many generators are ready to use. If yours is not, follow its manufacturer instructions or use an authorized generator service provider.

Using circuits within generator capacity

Your interlock powers the panel, not a fixed shortlist of circuits. Turn on only what the generator can support at the same time.

  • Start large motor loads one at a time.
  • If the generator breaker trips, reduce the load before resetting it.
  • Follow the generator manual for rated output and operating limits.

BPP installs and supports

  • House-side equipment: the installed inlet, interlock, and project wiring.
  • Project delivery: permit, required inspection, system test, and walkthrough.
  • Installed-system support: heat, damage, odor, sparks, or an interlock that will not move freely.

Generator owner responsibilities

  • Compatibility: the generator outlet, neutral setup, and model-specific requirements.
  • Operation and upkeep: placement, starting, fuel, load management, storage, and maintenance.
  • Generator service: the manufacturer or an authorized generator service provider handles generator inspection, configuration, repair, or modification.
Work covered by BPP: BPP's work is limited to the house-side equipment and services listed in the approved proposal. Generator selection, compatibility, operation, maintenance, inspection, repair, and modification are not included in BPP's work. If the generator setup is uncertain, do not connect it until the manufacturer or an authorized generator service provider confirms it.

Safety basis: generator manufacturer instructions, installed equipment instructions, and current CPSC guidance. This guide does not replace the owner manual for your exact generator.
